# Game-first asset production contract — unvalidated

**Status: unvalidated as a cross-category workflow.** Read before the applicable
construction module. This project authors runtime game assets by default, not CGI
render assets. The main skill and `AGENTS.md` retain approval and safety authority.
This contract neither authorizes game integration nor changes locked actor representations.

## 1. Separate destination from likeness

Game-first does not mean automatically cartoonish, flat shaded or inaccurate. Select
stylized/gameplay-read, reference-faithful or reconstruction independently of runtime
budget. Preserve the approved identifying features, required openings and motion in
every mode. Do not silently simplify a reference-faithful brief to meet an invented budget.

Default to a modest editable cage/profile and a runtime-conscious material plan. Dense
subdivision, sculpting, strands, displacement or film-scale textures require an explicit
purpose and approval: for example a separate bake source or supported hero close-up.
An approved high-resolution source is not the delivery mesh. Do not automatically create
a high/low pair when the budgeted base geometry and existing materials already suffice.

## 2. Agree the delivery envelope before geometry

Record in the existing construction brief/work card:

| Decision | Required information |
| --- | --- |
| Role and visibility | Background, interactive or hero; closest camera/screen coverage; views that can expose interiors/undersides |
| Repetition and platform | Expected simultaneous instances, target quality/device lane and any known scene bottleneck |
| Geometry | Proposed delivered triangles and exported vertices; silhouette/deformation areas needing density; source versus delivery counts |
| Materials and textures | Material primitives/slots, source reuse, map sizes/channels, transparency, shadow/reflection needs and export support |
| Behavior | Static/skinned/rigid needs, pivots, clips, collision/nav requirements and representation ownership |
| Evidence and scope | Shape approval, budget checks, export/clean import and separately approved in-game checks |

If a decision is unknown, propose a bounded assumption for approval or ask when it changes
the result. Do not invent engine limits, collider support or a universal triangle target.
Budget ceilings are not goals to fill. Material slots, Blender objects and draw calls
are different quantities; report what was actually measured.

## 3. Spend geometry where it contributes

Build required silhouette, depth/parallax, contact, holes and articulation. Reserve
loops for curvature and bends visible at the intended scale. Use smooth shading without
assuming more subdivision is necessary. Decide geometry versus material at the closest
required view, not a magnified diagnostic view alone.

Use existing texture/material sources where suitable. Prefer fine surface relief, grain,
printed graphics and nonstructural colour boundaries in materials; omit details explicitly
excluded by the brief. A texture cannot replace a required opening or deformed silhouette.
Do not add hidden interiors, joinery, engine internals or simulated cloth by default.
Do not remove protected source geometry or required closed-solid surfaces under this rule.

Test the hard interface before replication, including its expected budget. Prove one
module/member/appendage rather than multiplying excess density. Choose LODs, atlasing,
instances or compression only when justified and approved; they are not automatic new
dependencies or runtime systems. Reuse project catalog/store ownership and batching rules.

## 4. Preserve source; verify the delivery copy

Keep editable sources and accepted checkpoints. On a distinct copy, compare lower
subdivision, scoped simplification or retopology at intended screen scale and nearest
supported distance. Do not decimate an entire animated asset and assume skinning, shape
keys, UVs or material boundaries survived. Recheck the actual required poses/interfaces.
Stop when reduction breaks a required cue; propose a tradeoff rather than weakening it.

Record raw source, evaluated source and delivery/export counts separately. UV/normal
seams and material splits may increase runtime vertices. Measure the final triangulated
export and inspect a clean import with maps connected; cage vertices alone are not a
runtime budget. Report texture sizes and file size, not just triangles. Preserve all
existing approval/manifest/validator gates and destination-engine limitations.

## 5. Verify the claim, not just the number

After separately approved integration, route through `gummy-game-verification`. Inspect
actual game cameras, light/quality paths, repetitions, lifecycle and required interactions.
Use representative populations for measured CPU/GPU/resource claims. Fur/foliage alpha,
skinning, shadows, transmission and extra materials can cost more than the saved triangles.
An asset under budget can still perform badly or look wrong.

Distinguish **source editable / shape accepted / delivery budget met / export verified /
in-game verified**. Without runtime access, report that gap; do not claim game readiness
from a Blender render, a valid GLB or a polygon count. If role/budget requirements conflict,
present concrete alternatives for user approval. Do not replace the game stack or add a
dependency to conceal the issue. Keep the shared failed-repair ledger and cleanup rules.
